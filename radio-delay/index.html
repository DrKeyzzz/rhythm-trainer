Radio Delay

Hold back the radio call so it lines up with the game on TV.

1. Where's the radio coming from?

Best option on a computer or Chromebook (Chrome or Edge).
  1. Open the radio broadcast in another tab and start it playing.
  2. Press Start, choose that tab, and make sure “Also share tab audio” is on.
  3. The original tab goes quiet and you'll hear it here, delayed.
Paste a station's direct stream link (usually ends in .mp3, .aac or /stream). Some stations block this; if so, use Browser tab or Mic / cable.
Works on any device, including iPhone. Best: run a headphone cable from the phone playing the radio app into this device's mic/line-in jack. Or set the radio phone right next to this device's mic and play the delayed audio through headphones or a Bluetooth speaker so it doesn't echo back in.

2. Set the delay

0.0sec
Not running

Radio still ahead? Add seconds. Radio now behind the TV? Take some away. Pausing the TV? Pause the radio too and it picks up right where it stopped.

3. Or let it measure for you

Pick a clear moment, like a pitch or the crack of the bat. Tap the orange button the instant you hear it on the radio, then the purple button the instant you see it on TV. Either order works. Repeat a few times to fine-tune.

Tips

  • Keep this page open and in front. The screen stays on while it's running.
  • Your delay is saved, so next game it starts where you left it.
  • It holds up to 5 minutes of audio. After you raise the delay, the radio goes quiet until enough has built up. The badge shows how long that will take.
  • Streaming TV often drifts a little over a game. Tap ±0.2 when the call and the picture drift apart.
